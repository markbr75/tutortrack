TutorTrack admin guide

How to set up and configure a tutoring business on TutorTrack, and how the TutorTrack service itself is operated.

Last updated: 2026-10-22

About this guide

This guide has two parts:

Part A: organisation administrators

Owners and admins of a tutoring business: sign-up, settings, team and roles, payments, pricing, policies, communications, integrations, data, website, privacy and safeguarding.

Part B: platform operators

The people who run the TutorTrack service: the TutorTrack console, configuration, deployment, backups, monitoring and key rotation.

Day-to-day work (the calendar, clients, invoicing, payroll runs, the tutor app and the client portal) is in the user guide. The public REST API, OAuth apps and webhooks are in the API guide.

Basics worth knowing first

  • Your organisation is your subdomain. Each business lives at its own address, such as brightminds.tutortrack.app. Data from different organisations is kept apart at every level, down to the database. If you belong to several organisations, the switcher at the bottom of the menu moves between them.
  • Configuration lives in one place. Settings at the bottom of the sidebar opens the settings area. Its own menu follows the order of this guide: Organisation, Teaching and money, Communication, Data and website and Trust and safety.
  • What you see depends on your role. Menu items appear only if you have the permission they need. Each chapter names that permission (for example payments.provider.manage) and any plan the feature needs.
  • Everything is audited. Every change is recorded in the audit log with who made it.
  • Money is never edited after the fact. Issued invoices, credit notes, payments and approved pay items are permanent; mistakes are corrected with credit notes and adjustments.
  • Times are stored in UTC and shown in local time. Organisations, branches, people and lessons each carry a timezone; recurring lessons keep their local time when the clocks change.
  • Some options have no screen yet. Where this guide says "API only", an owner or admin changes the option through the settings API (see settings without a screen).

Setup checklist for going live

  1. Sign up and complete the onboarding wizard; set the currency and language carefully. Load demo data if you want to explore, and wipe it before you go live.
  2. Complete the organisation profile: legal name, timezone, contact details and, if you have several locations, branches.
  3. Set up the catalogue: subjects, services, tax rates, pay rates or tiers, discounts and packages. Use Price check to test.
  4. Set your cancellation policy and lesson report templates.
  5. Connect payments (Stripe, GoCardless or PayPal) and choose auto-pay timing and the payment methods each branch accepts. Review the billing options.
  6. Review notifications and reword the messages families receive. On Enterprise or with the add-on, set up a sending domain.
  7. Add your branding (logo, colours, email and invoice footers) and, if you have one, a custom domain.
  8. Import your existing data, or use Move from another system. Check the dry run before every import.
  9. Invite your team, give each person the right role, and decide whether to require two-factor authentication or single sign-on.
  10. Connect accounting and calendars and video, and turn on online booking or automations if you want them.
  11. Review the privacy centre: accept the data processing agreement, choose retention and children's privacy settings, and appoint your safeguarding leads.
  12. Add a payment method on Billing & plan before the 30-day trial ends.

Part A · Organisation administrators

Signing up and onboarding

Create the organisation

Go to the sign-up page (Create your TutorTrack account, "Free trial. No card needed."). Enter your name, email and password, the Business name, the Country and the Type of business (Sole trader, Small team, Agency, Tuition centre or Online school), accept the Terms of Service and select Create account.

  • Web address. Your organisation gets its own subdomain, made from the business name (for example bright-minds.tutortrack.app; -2 is added if the name is taken). The subdomain is how TutorTrack knows which organisation you are working in: every bookmark, invitation and portal link uses it.
  • Region. The country decides where your data is hosted. If it belongs to another region (UK, EU, US or Australia), the page links you to that region's sign-up. See data residency.
  • Password. At least 8 characters, not easy to guess and not found in known breaches.
  • Timezone. Taken from your browser; change it later in Settings.

After sign-up you land on the onboarding wizard on your new subdomain, and a Confirm your email message goes to your inbox.

Choose currency and language carefully

The wizard is the only screen that sets the organisation's currency and language. Prices, invoices and payments are recorded in that currency, and financial records cannot be edited after they are issued. Change them before you issue the first invoice, if at all.

Demo data

To explore before you add real records, select Explore with demo data at the bottom of the wizard. TutorTrack adds two services, three sample families with four students, two tutors, weekly lessons (three weeks past and four ahead, with reports), invoices in different states, payments, enquiries, a course, a class, homework and a review. Every sample record is tagged Sample data.

  • Sample data never sends emails or texts, runs automations, syncs to accounting or fires webhooks, and uses its own invoice numbers (DEMO-INV-…), so your real numbering is untouched.
  • Remove it from Settings › Demo data › Wipe demo data. The section only appears while sample data exists.
  • Wiping keeps any sample record that real data now points to (for example a real invoice for a sample family).
  • Loading and wiping need org.settings.manage and are both audited.

The Get started checklist

The home dashboard shows a Get started card with five steps: set up a service, add a student, schedule a lesson, connect Stripe (any payment provider counts) and send your first invoice. Steps tick themselves as you do them. Owners and admins can select Hide checklist.

General and terminology

Default lesson length (minutes) (default 60, 5–480) is used when you create jobs and lessons. Terminology renames the key words across the app: singular and plural for Tutor, Student, Client, Lesson and Job (for example "Teacher" and "Pupil").

Branches

Branches split a business into locations or divisions. Branch-scoped people only see the records in their branches, and some settings (lesson length, travel buffer, payment methods, pay period) can be overridden per branch.

  • Add a branch with Branch name, Code and Add branch. Archive works on every branch except the default.
  • Plan Multiple branches need the Agency plan or above, and the plan's branch limit applies (10 on Agency). Extra branches above those included are charged per branch.

Matching needs and scheduled progress reports

  • Gender preferences (Matching › Weights, matching.gender_preference, off by default; needs matching.settings.manage to see the tab and org.settings.manage to change it). Turn it on only where it is lawful for you to act on a family's preference for a tutor's gender. When it is on, staff can record a student's Tutor gender preference and matching only shows tutors of that gender (tutors with no gender recorded don't match). When it is off, a stored preference is ignored everywhere. Tutors' gender needs people.tutor.view_sensitive; see sensitive fields.
  • Exam board and SEN experience weights (Matching › Weights, 10 each). They only count when a search asks for them (the job's students have exam boards or SEN tags recorded, or staff add them), so other searches score as before.
  • Draft progress reports automatically (Learning › Progress reports, learning.progress_schedule: Don't draft automatically (default), Every month or At the end of each term). Monthly drafts last month's reports at 06:00 on the 1st; termly drafts them at 06:00 the day after a term (set up under Classes) ends, for students in that term's branch. Only missing reports are drafted, each for the student's main tutor, who is notified (Progress reports drafted for you (to the tutor)); staff who approve reports get a summary (Progress reports drafted on schedule). The schedule runs as a Temporal Schedule and pauses with the organisation.
  • Waitlist suggestions need no setting: staff with leads.waitlist.manage get the in-app notice A tutor has room for waiting students, which they can switch to email under their notification preferences.

TutorTrack support access

This section of Settings controls when TutorTrack's own support staff may look at your account. By default, support can view your account read-only when you raise a ticket; every visit is listed here and you are notified (TutorTrack support accessed your account, with their reason).

  1. To let support make changes, or to allow access when your organisation requires a grant, enter the number of Days (1–30, default 7).
  2. Tick Allow changes only if support needs to fix something for you.
  3. Select Grant access. The grant shows its end date; select Revoke to end it early.

Only the owner can grant or revoke access (support.access.manage); admins can see the list. To require a grant for every support visit, turn on security.support_access_requires_grant (API only).

Close the account

The owner can close the organisation from Settings › Close account: give a reason, type the subdomain and your password, and select Close account permanently. The subscription is cancelled, you get a full export by email, and the data is kept for 30 days and then deleted.

Team and invitations

Team lists every member of the organisation with their role. Viewing needs team.view; inviting needs team.invite; changing roles and removing people needs team.manage.

Invite someone

  1. Under Invite someone, enter the Email.
  2. Choose the Role (default Tutor). Only an owner can invite another owner.
  3. Select Send invitation. The link is valid for 7 days and opens on your subdomain; people who already have a TutorTrack account sign in with the invited email.
  4. Manage open invitations under Pending invitations (the five most recent show; Show all lists the rest): Resend creates a new link (the old one stops working) and Revoke cancels it.

Tutors, clients and students are usually invited from their records (see the user guide); the Team page is mostly for staff.

Change a role or remove someone

  • Change a member's role from the drop-down next to their name (Owner, Admin, Branch manager, Coordinator, Finance, Tutor, Client, Student). For a mix of permissions, use custom roles.
  • Remove ends their access straight away. Their history and audit trail stay.
  • Nobody can change or remove themselves, and the organisation must always keep at least one owner.
  • Branch membership, job titles and suspending (rather than removing) a member are available through the API only.
Plan limits

Tutors (onboarding, active and restricted) count against the plan's tutor limit: 1 on Solo, 15 on Team, unlimited on Agency and Enterprise.

View as another member

To see exactly what a tutor, client or student sees, select View as on their row (needs impersonation.start). A banner shows You're viewing as {name}. and Read-only.; select Stop viewing as to return. Everything you do is audited with you recorded as the impersonator. Making changes while viewing as someone needs impersonation.write, which only the owner has by default.

Branch managers and organisation-wide actions

Branch scope limits records that belong to a branch. Actions that have no branch, such as inviting team members, editing roles, single sign-on and View as, are not limited by it today, so give the Branch manager role only to people you would trust with those.

Recruitment and HR permissions

Recruitment, compliance, probation reviews, performance notes and offboarding are given only to owners and admins by default. To let someone else run them, make a custom role with recruitment.application.view/edit/decide, recruitment.pipeline.manage (openings and forms), compliance.view/verify (verifying and requesting documents), recruitment.performance.view/manage (probation reviews and notes) and recruitment.offboarding.manage.

Custom roles

  1. Under New role, enter a Role name and choose what to Start from (Admin, Branch manager, Coordinator, Finance or Tutor), then select Create role.
  2. Choose the role under Custom roles. Its permissions are grouped by area, each saying how many are allowed (for example "2 of 4 allowed"); open an area to change it. Set each permission to No, Their own, Their branches or Everyone, and select Save changes at the foot of the list, which counts your unsaved changes. Areas you changed stay open. Hover a permission to see its code name (for example people.client.view).
  3. Under Team members with this role, tick the staff and tutors who should have it.
  • Someone with custom roles gets everything those roles allow instead of their built-in role's permissions. The built-in role still decides whether they are staff or a portal user and whether the staff 2FA rule applies.
  • Owners and portal users cannot have custom roles, owner-only permissions cannot be granted, and nobody can change their own roles.
  • Archive removes a role from everyone who has it.
  • A custom role is a copy: it does not get permissions added in later releases. Review your custom roles after new features ship.
  • The only way to make someone other than the owner a Designated Safeguarding Lead is a custom role that allows safeguarding.dsl.
  • The referral, affiliate and review reports need two permissions: reporting.sales.view and the matching reviews and referrals one (growth.referral.view, growth.affiliate.view or growth.review.view), whose scope also limits the figures. Coordinators get all three reports; Finance gets the referral and affiliate reports but not Review scores. Scheduled reports only go to people who hold both.

Single sign-on and sign-in security

Organisation single sign-on

Settings › Single sign-on lets your team sign in with your own identity provider (Microsoft Entra ID, Okta, Google Workspace and others) over OpenID Connect or SAML 2.0. Needs security.sso.manage.

Plan Turning it on needs the Enterprise plan ("Single sign-on is part of the Enterprise plan."), for both protocols.

  1. Choose the Protocol and a Sign-in button label.
  2. Enter your Email domains, separated by commas (up to 20).
  3. For SAML, copy the Entity ID (audience) and Reply URL (ACS) from the Give your identity provider panel below the form into your identity provider, then enter its entity ID, sign-in URL and signing certificate (PEM). For OpenID Connect, give the provider the Redirect URL shown in that panel, then enter the Issuer URL, Client ID and Client secret.
  4. Optionally tick Add new people automatically (new people join as Tutor unless you change the default role through the API) and Require single sign-on for these domains (no passwords or email links).
  5. Tick Turn on single sign-on and select Save. Test with a second browser before you require it.

Enforcement applies only to the listed domains on your organisation's address. Encrypted assertions, identity-provider-initiated SAML, single logout and SCIM are not supported. Separately, anyone can use the Google, Microsoft and Apple buttons on the sign-in page.

Two-factor authentication and sessions

  • Each person sets up 2FA (authenticator app or passkey, with 10 one-time recovery codes) on their Account page. Passkeys do not work on custom domains.
  • To require 2FA for every staff role, including tutors, turn on security.require_mfa_for_staff (API only). People without 2FA are then asked to enrol before they can do anything else.
  • Idle staff are signed out after 8 hours; portal users stay signed in for 30 days with "remember me". Both are adjustable (see settings without a screen).
  • After 5 failed sign-ins in 15 minutes, each further attempt is delayed. A password reset signs out every session.
  • Each person gets an alert email when they sign in from a new device, and when a sign-in comes from a different country within 2 hours of the last one (impossible travel).

Audit log

Audit log records every change made through TutorTrack: who did it (and who they were viewing as), when, from which IP address and device, the record and the field-level changes. It is append-only: nobody, including TutorTrack staff, can edit or delete entries. Needs audit.view (owner and admin).

  • Filter by Search (record description), Who (email), Action, From and To, then Apply, in the filter row above the log.
  • Reading an entry: each row shows the action with who made it and when beneath, the record (with its type) and the changes, one "field before → after" line each (the first six). On phones the record joins that line too.
  • Export with Export CSV at the top right (needs audit.export; up to 50,000 rows). Exports are audited too, and five exports by one person in an hour sends the owners a security alert.
  • Sensitive values such as bank details are shown as [REDACTED].
  • Reads of sensitive data are logged with the action read: tax IDs, login history, student sensitive data, tutor bank and tax details, payment files, background-check results, data-request exports, safeguarding cases, data exports, webhook secrets and lesson recordings.
  • Safeguarding entries are only shown to Designated Safeguarding Leads.
  • Entries are kept for at least 7 years.

Trial, payment and plan changes

  • Trial. 30 days with Agency features from sign-up. Afterwards you continue on the plan that fits your business type (sole trader: Solo; small team: Team; others: Agency) if you added a payment method. Without one, the account becomes read-only until you choose a plan. A card added during the trial keeps the remaining days.
  • Payment. Add payment method and Payment details and invoices open Stripe's secure pages. Your TutorTrack invoices are listed under Invoices.
  • Upgrades (and monthly to annual) apply at once with a prorated charge. Downgrades and annual to monthly apply at the end of the billing period. If you are over the smaller plan's limits (tutors, branches, active students, storage), the dialog lists what to change first.
  • Usage shows each limit as "{used} of {allowed}", the billable tutors this period and an estimate of the next invoice.
  • SMS and AI credits. Buy packs of texts or AI credits, and choose whether to top up automatically or keep sending when credits run out (charged next period).

Tenant health

Each night (03:30 UTC) every organisation gets a score from 0 to 100, built from usage depth (30%), the active-tutor trend (20%), the payments trend (20%), reliability (10%), support conversations (10%) and NPS (10%). The three database roles

Tenant isolation is enforced twice: in the application (a tenant-scoped default manager) and in PostgreSQL (RLS policies keyed on the organisation set for each transaction). The second layer only works if the application cannot bypass it, so TutorTrack connects with three roles (ADR 0003):

DATABASE_OWNER_URLThe table owner (RDS master user)Only the migrate task. migrate picks it up automatically.

At start-up the app checks that its default role cannot bypass RLS (DB_RLS_ROLE_CHECK, default error: refuse to start). Creating a BYPASSRLS role on RDS needs a superuser-equivalent; check this on the first apply, and create tutortrack_platform by hand once if the master user cannot.

Celery or Temporal

Domain events are written to an outbox table inside the same transaction as the change and dispatched by the worker to handlers, notifications, automations, webhooks and integrations. Multi-step, timer-based or approval-based processes run as Temporal workflows with tenant-prefixed IDs; per-organisation recurring processes (invoice runs, pay-run cut-offs, retention purges) are Temporal Schedules. If Temporal is unavailable, starts and signals fail and the outbox retries them, so nothing is lost. Failed or stuck workflows are handled from the workflow console.

Deploying

The baseline AWS stack is described in Terraform under infra/terraform/ (VPC, RDS, ElastiCache, S3, ECS Fargate, ALB, CloudFront, Secrets Manager, ECR, KMS and monitoring). Initialise it with an environment backend file and plan with the matching variables file (env/staging.s3.tfbackend, env/staging.tfvars).

  1. Merge to main with CI green. CI runs the same checks as make check, including the guide coverage check.
  2. The deploy workflow builds the image, pushes it to ECR and runs the migrate task (ensure_db_roles, then migrate). Migrations are expand/contract, so the running version keeps working against the new schema.
  3. New task definitions roll out to web, worker, beat and temporal-worker. Old Temporal workers drain before they stop.
  4. Watch the CloudWatch dashboard for 15 minutes (5xx rate, p95 latency, outbox lag) and check Sentry for new issues tagged with the release.
  5. If an alarm fires, roll back the services. Never roll back a migration: write a forward fix.

Full steps: deploy runbook and rollback runbook.

Not yet proven against real infrastructure

The Terraform has not been applied to a live account. Per-region stacks for data residency, tenant custom-domain DNS, a ClamAV service, a status page provider, alarms on workflow failures and an ASGI web service (needed for the realtime notification stream; see the realtime notifications runbook) all still need real accounts or infrastructure work.

Backups and restore

  • Point-in-time recovery: RDS keeps 35 days (RPO 5 minutes or less).
  • Snapshots: AWS Backup takes daily snapshots of RDS and the uploads bucket (35 days) and copies them to a vault in a separate backup account (365 days).
  • Target: RTO 4 hours or less. Prove it every quarter with the restore drill.
  • One organisation: restore a scratch copy, then export that organisation with python manage.py tenant_dump <slug> --output <file> (it reads through the BYPASSRLS role) and recreate the lost records through the app's services or API so audit, events and invoices stay consistent.
Do not edit financial records during a restore

Issued invoices, payments and credit notes are immutable. Recreate missing records through the services, and correct mistakes with credit notes. After a full restore, replay provider webhooks for the lost window; payments are idempotent by provider reference.

Monitoring and alerts

Alarms go to an email SNS topic, and paging alarms to PagerDuty. The beat service publishes application metrics (outbox lag, dead letters, webhook backlogs, Celery queue depth); missing metrics count as breaching, so if every TutorTrack/* alarm fires at once, check that beat is running.

Encryption keys and rotation

Secrets and the most sensitive personal data (MFA secrets, OAuth and provider tokens, bank details, tax IDs, safeguarding notes, SEN and medical notes, DBS numbers) are encrypted in the application with Fernet keys from FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEYS, newest first. With FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KMS_KEY_ID set, the keys are AWS KMS data keys stored in wrapped form (envelope encryption). Temporal workflow payloads use separate AES-256-GCM keys in TEMPORAL_PAYLOAD_KEYS.

  1. Generate a new key: python manage.py generate_encryption_key (add --temporal --key-id k2 for a Temporal payload key). With KMS configured, only the wrapped key is printed.
  2. Put the new key first in the variable and keep the old keys after it, then deploy. New values are written with the new key; old values still decrypt.
  3. Re-encrypt existing data: python manage.py rotate_encryption_keys. It runs on the platform (BYPASSRLS) connection so every organisation's rows are included, and prints a count per field.
  4. When the count is complete and nothing has failed, remove the old field key from FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEYS and deploy again. Keep old Temporal payload keys until no running workflow history still uses them.
Losing a key loses the data

A value encrypted with a key that is no longer listed cannot be read. Never remove a key before rotate_encryption_keys has finished. Rotate keys after any suspected compromise as part of incident response (docs/compliance/incident-response.md).
